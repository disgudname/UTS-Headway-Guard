GTFS & GTFS-Realtime

A static GTFS schedule and GTFS-Realtime feeds for UVA Transit's fixed-route bus service (University Transit Service), built from the block packages UVA Transit operates to rather than from the AVL vendor's export. All IDs are shared between the static and realtime feeds. The feeds are public, need no key, and send Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *.

Static GTFS

Scope

  • Agency: UVA Transit (agency_id UTS), America/New_York. Fare-free (a single free fare, price 0.00).
  • Routes: Gold Line, Green Line, Green Loop, Orange Line, Orange Loop, Silver Line, Night Pilot, Purple Line. Route colors are the operator's.
  • Excluded: game-day lot shuttles, charter, training and test routes. These exist in the AVL system but are not public scheduled service.
  • Files: agency, routes, stops, trips, stop_times, frequencies, calendar_dates, shapes, fare_attributes, feed_info. There is no calendar.txt: every service day is listed explicitly in calendar_dates.txt.

How the schedule is built

  • Times come from UVA Transit's block packages: each block's sequence of timestop visits. Every pair of consecutive visits is one GTFS trip, so a block's day is a chain of short trips joined by block_id. Timestops carry the published time (timepoint=1).
  • Intermediate stops (timepoint=0) are spaced by the AVL system's planned stop-to-stop run times, compressed when the block package allows less time than planned. Any remaining time is layover at the next timestop: a bus arriving early holds there.
  • Pattern changes follow the block package's route-change notes. Most lines switch to an evening pattern around 18:00, published as a separate route where riders know it by a different name (Green Line → Green Loop, Orange Line → Orange Loop) and as a different shape on the same route for Gold.
  • End of service follows each block's out-of-service instructions: the last trip ends at the last stop that block serves before returning to the lot.
  • Purple Line runs on headway-based dispatching (anti-bunching), not a timetable, so it is published as frequencies.txt with exact_times=0, in bands derived from the planned service levels (roughly every 3–10 minutes depending on time of day, Monday–Friday).
  • Service days come from the UTS service calendar. Recess days run the recess timetables for the lines that have one; "No Service" days have no trips; "Health Commuter" days run the Purple Line only. The feed ends on the last day the published calendar covers (feed_end_date) rather than assuming regular service beyond it.

GTFS-Realtime

Vehicle Positions

  • Every bus the AVL system reports on a published route, with a GPS fix less than 90 s old.
  • vehicle.id and vehicle.label are the bus number painted on the vehicle (e.g. 19232).
  • Trip assignment: the candidate trips are those of the bus's dispatched block on its current route that run today. The one whose scheduled time at the bus's next stop is closest to its predicted arrival there wins, within 15 minutes early to 30 minutes late. A bus with no current trip (e.g. before its first trip of the day) is published with route_id only.
  • stop_id / current_stop_sequence name the next stop, with current_status IN_TRANSIT_TO.
  • Purple Line trips are UNSCHEDULED and carry a start_time (required for exact_times=0 frequency trips), worked back from the bus's position in its loop. All other trips are SCHEDULED.

Occupancy

occupancy_status and occupancy_percentage come from the automatic passenger counters. The percentage is riders divided by the vehicle's rated capacity and can exceed 100. Status thresholds depend on the vehicle type:

Trip Updates

  • Predictions come from our own arrival-prediction engine, not the AVL vendor's: live position along the route, learned stop-to-stop travel times by day of week and hour, and the scheduled holds at timestops.
  • Each update gives absolute arrival.time (= departure.time) for the stops remaining on the trip, strictly increasing. A stop the engine cannot predict is omitted, not estimated.
  • Each bus also gets updates for its block's next trip when it continues on the same route, so riders see predictions for a bus that is still finishing its previous trip.
Because of those next-trip updates, a combined check of both realtime feeds reports W003 ("ID in one feed missing from the other") for trip IDs that are in Trip Updates but not yet in Vehicle Positions. This is expected, and the rule's own documentation says it can be ignored in this case.

Service Alerts

  • Published from the active, public system notices dispatch maintains.
  • informed_entity: the whole agency, specific stop_ids, or route_ids, as the notice is targeted. Notices about services outside this feed are left out.
  • header_text is the short form, description_text the full text. active_period follows the notice's start/end. cause and effect are UNKNOWN_*; severity_level is INFO, WARNING or SEVERE.

Validation

  • Static: MobilityData GTFS Validator 8.0.1, US rules: no errors, no warnings.
  • Realtime: MobilityData GTFS-Realtime Validator, run on consecutive live snapshots of each feed against the static feed: no errors. The only warnings seen are the W003 case above and, for a bus not yet on a trip, a route_id-only trip descriptor.

Last checked October 2026, against the production feeds.

Known limitations

  • Times at intermediate stops are interpolated from planned run times. Only timestops are published times.
  • The Purple Line's headways are planned service levels. Actual spacing is managed live.
  • Exam and summer service use the regular timetables. Recess has its own timetables for the lines that have one.
  • Stops, stop locations and route shapes come from the AVL vendor. When a stop moves there, it moves here too.
  • The feed horizon is only as far ahead as the published service calendar.
